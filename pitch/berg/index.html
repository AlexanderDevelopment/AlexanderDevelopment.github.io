Elevator Pitch

A grounded co-op climbing adventure set on the Zugspitze.

BERG is a first-person co-op game about reaching the summit in modern alpine conditions. Players start at the base of the mountain, choose routes, deliver supplies, complete tasks and climb upward while overcoming environmental obstacles.

The core interaction is simple and readable: control each hand, manage grip, secure the rope, monitor oxygen and keep moving. Depth comes from route choice, weather pressure, oxygen management, cold exposure and the presence of other players on the same mountain.

Moment-to-Moment Loop

The climb is built around clear physical decisions.

BERG translates climbing into a readable gameplay loop. Players read the route, climb by moving each hand separately, attach themselves to rope anchors and monitor their body state under changing alpine conditions.

01

Read the route

Players follow marked climbing lines, evaluate the next section and choose when to continue, rest or change approach.

02

Move each hand

Separate left and right hand inputs create the core climbing rhythm.

03

Attach to anchors

Players secure themselves to fixed rope points to reduce fall risk and create safer progress.

04

Monitor body state

Cold, oxygen and stamina affect movement speed, grip reliability and recovery.

Failure model

Fall, ragdoll, freeze, recover. Mistakes do not delete the run. Players can lose time, position and route progress, but return to a recoverable state or respawn from the checkpoint.

Weather pressure

Changing weather supports longer 60+ minute attempts. Strong wind can interfere with movement and drown out voice chat. Snowstorms reduce visibility and can separate players, creating tense moments where teammates must call out to find each other again.

Mountain Structure

One mountain, multiple routes to the same summit.

All players start at the base of the mountain. The mountain contains several rope-marked routes that all lead toward the same summit, but each route creates a different risk profile. At the beginning, only one route is clearly available. As players progress, they unlock new routes and learn which lines are safer, faster or more demanding under specific conditions.

NPC tasks give players practical goals during the climb. Completing tasks rewards currency that can be spent on better oxygen tanks, improved hand stamina, longer ropes, winch-based rope tools, radios and other climbing gadgets.

There is one main checkpoint in the middle of the mountain. It reduces frustration while preserving the feeling of one continuous climb.

Route logic

Base -> route choice -> middle checkpoint -> summit push

Quest examples

  • Deliver an oxygen tank to an intermediate camp
  • Deliver supplies to a high-altitude tent camp
  • Bring down an NPC suffering from altitude sickness
  • Evacuate climbers stranded on the route

Simulation Pillars

Realism is expressed through readable gameplay systems.

BERG should feel grounded and atmospheric without becoming a hardcore climbing simulator. The player has a clear HUD for important survival and movement indicators, while the mountain systems directly affect moment-to-moment decisions.

Social Mountain

Multiplayer turns the mountain into a shared expedition space.

BERG is not limited to a closed four-player party. The concept is built around a cloud-hosted Photon server where multiple teams share the same mountain, cross paths on routes, hear nearby players through distance-based voice chat and create organic rescue, supply and cooperation moments.

Public base

Players gather, choose routes, accept tasks and form groups.

Route traffic

Teams cross each other on easier and more technical climbing lines.

Emergent help

Supplies, rescue actions and rope support become social interactions.

Summit stories / leaderboard

The server records who reached the summit and under which conditions.

Progression and Retention

Replayability comes from upgrades, harder routes and shared stories.

BERG is designed around repeated attempts to reach the summit under different conditions. Players improve their equipment, unlock more demanding routes and return to the mountain with different friends to experience new failures, rescues and summit stories.

Progression

  • Better oxygen tanks
  • Improved hand stamina
  • Longer ropes
  • Radios and climbing gadgets
  • New route access

Retention

  • Try a harder route
  • Return with another group
  • Recover from failed attempts
  • Create memorable co-op moments
  • Reach the summit under different conditions

Business model

Premium Steam

Market Position

Climbing games show clear demand, but PC still has room for a grounded social alpine title.

BERG is positioned between PEAK and more realistic Roblox climbing experiences such as K2 Climbing Simulator. The goal is a dedicated premium PC product, not a mod or platform-limited experience.

PEAK

A strong reference for social climbing with broad streamer-friendly readability. Its tone is closer to fantasy, friendslop and comedy than to an atmospheric climbing simulation.

BERG

A dedicated premium PC climbing adventure set in the Bavarian Alps. BERG focuses on grounded atmosphere, route planning, weather pressure, proximity voice and shared-server expeditions.

Roblox K2 Climbing Simulator

A proof that players respond to climb-to-summit progression and public mountain status. Roblox shows the demand, while BERG translates the appeal into a dedicated PC Steam product.

BERG differenceBERG combines PEAK's social readability with K2's climb-to-summit progression, then moves both toward a grounded PC Steam experience: Zugspitze setting, weather pressure, proximity voice and shared-server expeditions.
